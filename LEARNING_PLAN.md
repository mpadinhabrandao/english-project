# Learning plan — all-round English, 15 minutes a day

**Learner:** software developer, native Portuguese speaker, ~A2 (to be confirmed in week 1), two A1/A2 courses in progress.
**Goal:** confident everyday and work English — speaking, writing, listening, reading. No exam; CEFR levels are the milestones (A2 → B1 → B2) and Cambridge's free tools are the measuring sticks.
**Budget:** 15 min/day of active practice, 6 days a week. Ambient input (podcasts, series, working in English) on top — that's where the hours come from.
**Block 1:** Monday 14 September → Sunday 6 December 2026 (12 weeks). Then re-test and plan block 2.

## 1. How this plan thinks

Six principles. Everything below follows from them.

1. **Consistency beats intensity.** 15 min × 6 days does more than 2 hours on Sunday, because memory consolidates *between* sessions. Miss a day → do tomorrow. Never catch up.
2. **Input you understand ~90%.** Listening and reading at the right level is the biggest driver of vocabulary and grammar. Too easy is fine; too hard is wasted time.
3. **Output every day, corrected.** A few sentences a day are enough — if they get corrected and the errors get logged. This is what turns knowledge into use.
4. **Errors are the syllabus.** Instead of a grammar book from page 1, `ERROR_BANK.md` decides what gets taught. Portuguese speakers make a predictable set of errors (catalogue in the skill); we hunt those first.
5. **Retrieval, spaced.** Warm-ups re-test yesterday's and last week's patterns. Re-reading rules doesn't work; producing them does.
6. **Real life is the material.** PR descriptions, Slack messages, the bug you fixed, your weekend. No invented exercises when a real text exists.

### About the hours — read this once

Cambridge's published estimates put the step from A2 to B1 at roughly 150–200 guided learning hours. Twelve weeks of 15-minute sessions is about 18 hours. So the sessions do a specific job: build the habit, fix the recurring errors, keep you producing every day. The *level* moves with ambient input — 30 min/day of podcasts, series or reading in English adds ~40 hours over the same 12 weeks at no study cost. Both matter. Only one of them costs you "study time".

## 2. Week 1 — level check + habit (don't skip)

You asked to start from the beginning. We do — but by *testing* A1/A2 instead of re-studying it.

- **Day 1 (~25 min, replaces the session):** [Cambridge Test your English](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/test-your-english/), free. Write the level in `PROGRESS.md`.
- **Day 2:** write 100–150 words ("My job and why I'm learning English") in [Write & Improve](https://writeandimprove.com/), free — it returns a CEFR estimate of your writing. Record it.
- **Day 3:** record a 60-second voice note in English about your week. Don't judge it; keep the file. Week 12 will compare.
- **Days 1–28, inside the warm-ups — the A1–A2 sweep:** the coach tests 3 checklist items a day by making you *produce* them (`.claude/skills/english-coach/references/a1-a2-checklist.md`, 44 items). ✅ after two correct productions; ❌ goes to the error bank and becomes a micro-lesson. In about 4 weeks you will have covered "the beginning" completely, spending time only on real gaps.
- Keep the two courses if you enjoy them, as *input*. Don't do their exercises on top of the session; the session already covers output.

## 3. The 15-minute session — the core loop

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Warm-up | 3 retrieval items: 2 error-bank patterns + 1 vocab item (or 3 sweep items in weeks 1–4) |
| 2–10 | Main task | Depends on the weekday (below). You produce; the coach corrects — max 3 fixes |
| 10–14 | Micro-lesson | The most important fix from today: one-line *why* with the Portuguese contrast + 3 production items |
| 14–15 | Log | The coach gives a `LOG` line + error-bank rows; you paste them into `PROGRESS.md` / `ERROR_BANK.md` |

Say **"daily session"** to the coach. If you have a real text to send, paste it with **"check this"** — that *is* the session for the day.

## 4. The week

| Day | Mode | Main task | Why this day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Write | 5–7 sentences: your weekend / your plan for the week → corrected | Fresh start, easy content |
| Tue | Read | A short text at your level (+1) with 5 target words → 3 questions | Vocabulary in context |
| Wed | Speak | Dictate with the phone mic → paste → corrected for *spoken* English → conversation | Speaking is the scariest; midweek, low stakes |
| Thu | Grammar | One pattern from the error bank → 4 production items | Only what you actually get wrong |
| Fri | Listen | Retell a podcast or video you heard this week in 3 sentences + an opinion | Turns ambient input into output |
| Sat | Free | English chat about something you enjoyed. No corrections unless you ask | Pleasure keeps the habit alive |
| Sun | Review (10 min) | Quiz the top 3 patterns, mark mastered, pick next week's ONE focus, tidy the files | Spacing + planning |

Missed a day? Skip it. The week is a rotation, not a checklist.

## 5. Ambient input — free time, not "study"

Pick two and make them automatic:

- **Phone, laptop and IDE in English.** Zero effort, hundreds of exposures a day.
- **Commute podcast.** A2–B1: *English Learning for Curious Minds* (slow, transcripts), BBC *6 Minute English*. B1+: any tech podcast you'd listen to anyway (Syntax, The Changelog, Software Engineering Daily) — you know the domain, so comprehension is high.
- **Series with English subtitles**, not Portuguese. Rewatch something you know. Language Reactor (browser extension) gives dual subtitles and a dictionary on Netflix/YouTube.
- **Read what you already read** — docs, READMEs, changelogs, Hacker News — but read the comments too; informal English lives there.
- **Work in English.** Commit messages, PR descriptions, Slack. Then get them corrected: `work` mode here, or `lang-tutor` / `english-coach` in Claude Code, so you're corrected *while* coding at zero extra time.
- **Human conversation (optional, from ~week 6):** one 30-minute italki or Cambly conversation a fortnight. Bring your error bank; ask the tutor to hit those patterns.

Log rough input minutes weekly. It's the number that predicts your level in December.

## 6. Roadmap — 12 weeks

| Weeks | Focus | You'll be able to… | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Habit + level check; sweep starts | Do the session on autopilot; know your real level | Placement, Write & Improve, voice note recorded |
| 3–6 | **Consolidate A2:** past narration, articles, prepositions, question formation, present continuous vs simple, ~300 work/life words | Tell what happened yesterday at work, in order, without Portuguese word order | Sweep A1 100% ✅, A2 ≥ 70% ✅; 5 patterns mastered |
| 7–10 | **B1 entry:** problem → cause → fix; future forms; comparisons; first conditional; connectors (*although, however, so, because*); meeting and phone phrases; 20 phrasal verbs | Explain a bug and its fix to a non-technical person; take part in a stand-up in English | Write & Improve reads B1 on a familiar topic; 8+ patterns mastered |
| 11–12 | Consolidate + re-test | 2 minutes of unscripted speaking without stopping | Repeat placement, Write & Improve, voice note. Compare. Plan block 2 |

Left out on purpose: perfect tenses beyond the basics, second/third conditionals, the passive, reported speech. They come in block 2, once the A2 base stops leaking.

## 8. Rules of engagement — for you and the coach

- Max 3 corrections per text. The other errors will come around again; that's what the bank is for.
- English first. Portuguese only for the one-line *why*.
- One focus per week. Not three.
- Review before new content. If the warm-up shows a pattern still leaking, the micro-lesson goes there.
- Missed sessions are not debt.
- Every 4 weeks, adjust *this file*, not just your effort. If Wednesday speaking never happens, change Wednesday.

## 9. What success looks like at week 12

- 5+ sessions a week in most weeks (streak in `PROGRESS.md`).
- 10 patterns in **Mastered**; the top-3 active patterns are new ones, not the September seeds.
- Write & Improve reads B1 on a work topic.
- The week-12 voice note is longer, faster and has fewer pauses than the week-1 one.
- You wrote your PRs and Slack messages in English all block, without dreading it.
